# MetaProp Mandate For Missed Deals Review

## Target Stage

- Early: pre-seed, seed, Series A.
- Growth: Series B, Series C, Series D, Series E, growth, late-stage.
- MetaProp mandate covers pre-seed through growth.
- Do not use `Unknown` for stage. If the newsletter does not state the round, make the best defensible `Early` or `Growth` judgment from round size, financing language, and public evidence of prior financings.
- When stage is unclear, search for prior financings before appending or refreshing:
  - Prior or current pre-seed, seed, or Series A evidence -> `Early`.
  - Prior Series B or later, strategic growth capital, follow-on debt after a growth round, or IPO-track/company-scale evidence -> `Growth`.
  - If no financing history can be found, use amount as a fallback: rounds below $25M are usually `Early`; rounds of $25M or more are usually `Growth`, unless public evidence says the round is Series A.

## Target Sectors

Classify `Fit? = Yes` when the company is clearly in one of these:

- PropTech
- Construction Tech
- Built World Software
- Real Estate Technology
- Property Management Software
- Facilities Management
- Smart Buildings
- AEC Software
- Real Estate Fintech
- Climate / Building Efficiency
- Energy management for buildings
- Infrastructure software for real estate, construction, buildings, or urban systems
- Home services or residential real estate workflow software
- Insurance / risk for real estate or built assets
- Logistics / industrial real estate software

Use provisional `Fit? = Maybe` internally when:

- The sector is built-world adjacent but not clearly core.
- Geography or customer market is unclear.
- The product could apply to real estate, construction, infrastructure, facilities, energy use in buildings, or urban systems, but the source does not confirm it.

Independent verification must resolve these to final Yes or No before publication. A specific source-backed built-world product, customer, or use case can justify a borderline Yes; a merely hypothetical application cannot. Record uncertainty in the audit reason rather than leaving Maybe in the completed sheet.

Classify `Fit? = No` when clearly outside mandate:

- Biotech
- Consumer social
- Gaming
- Crypto
- Pure e-commerce
- Non-built-world healthcare
- International-only outside US/Canada
- Generic horizontal software with no plausible built-world relevance

Append only final Yes deals. The completed sheet must have no Maybe rows.

## Target Geography

- Target geographies: United States and Canada.
- The sheet `Geography` field should be the company city/metro, plus state/province when available, e.g. `San Francisco, CA` or `Montreal, QC`.
- Do not use country-only values in `Geography` unless city is not available after enrichment.
- If city/geography is missing but source evidence establishes strong built-world relevance, route internally as Maybe for verification. Unknown geography alone need not exclude a strong case; clearly international-only companies remain No. Make a final Yes/No decision and document any residual geography uncertainty.
- A foreign headquarters alone does not prove international-only operations. Distinguish unknown US/Canadian market reach from explicit evidence of an exclusively international market; apply the evidence-backed borderline inclusion policy and record any remaining market uncertainty.
